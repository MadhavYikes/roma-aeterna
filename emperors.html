THE IMPERIAL SUCCESSION

Emperors

Major rulers, minor rulers and the short-lived claimants who shaped the Roman imperial succession. Click any emperor for a dedicated page.

27 BC–14 AD · Julio-Claudian

Augustus

First emperor — Established the Principate after the civil wars and reorganized the state.

14–37 · Julio-Claudian

Tiberius

Second emperor — Consolidated Augustus' settlement and maintained the imperial frontiers.

37–41 · Julio-Claudian

Caligula

Third emperor — A short and turbulent reign ended with his assassination in 41.

41–54 · Julio-Claudian

Claudius

Fourth emperor — Expanded Roman administration and began the conquest of Britain.

54–68 · Julio-Claudian

Nero

Last Julio-Claudian — His fall ended the Julio-Claudian dynasty and precipitated civil war.

68–69 · Year of the Four Emperors

Galba

Civil-war emperor — First of the four emperors of 69.

69 · Year of the Four Emperors

Otho

Civil-war emperor — Ruled for only a few months before defeat by Vitellius.

69 · Year of the Four Emperors

Vitellius

Civil-war emperor — Defeated by Vespasian's armies in the civil war of 69.

69–79 · Flavian

Vespasian

Flavian founder — Restored finances and began the Flavian Amphitheatre.

79–81 · Flavian

Titus

Flavian emperor — Completed the Colosseum and ruled after the eruption of Vesuvius.

81–96 · Flavian

Domitian

Last Flavian — Strengthened administration and frontier defense.

96–98 · Nerva–Antonine

Nerva

Adoptive emperor — Adopted Trajan and initiated the adoptive succession.

98–117 · Nerva–Antonine

Trajan

Conqueror — Annexed Dacia and brought the empire to its greatest territorial extent.

117–138 · Nerva–Antonine

Hadrian

Consolidator — Travelled throughout the empire and emphasized defensible frontiers.

138–161 · Nerva–Antonine

Antoninus Pius

Stable reign — Presided over a comparatively peaceful and prosperous reign.

161–180 · Nerva–Antonine

Marcus Aurelius

Philosopher-emperor — Ruled during major frontier wars and wrote the Meditations.

161–169 · Nerva–Antonine

Lucius Verus

Co-emperor — Co-ruled with Marcus Aurelius and was associated with the eastern war.

180–192 · Nerva–Antonine

Commodus

Last Antonine — His reign ended the Antonine dynasty.

193 · Year of the Five Emperors

Pertinax

Short-lived emperor — Attempted reform before being killed by Praetorian Guards.

193 · Year of the Five Emperors

Didius Julianus

Short-lived emperor — Became emperor after the notorious Praetorian auction.

193–211 · Severan

Septimius Severus

Severan founder — Won the civil wars of 193 and founded the Severan dynasty.

198–217 · Severan

Caracalla

Severan emperor — Issued the Constitutio Antoniniana in 212.

209–211 · Severan

Geta

Co-emperor — Co-ruled with Caracalla before being killed in the dynastic struggle.

218–222 · Severan

Elagabalus

Severan emperor — A young emperor strongly associated with the Syrian cult of Elagabal.

222–235 · Severan

Severus Alexander

Last Severan — His assassination ended the Severan dynasty.

235–238 · Crisis of the Third Century

Maximinus Thrax

Soldier-emperor — His accession begins the traditional Crisis of the Third Century.

238–244 · Crisis of the Third Century

Gordian III

Young emperor — Ruled during the conflict with Persia and died on campaign.

244–249 · Crisis of the Third Century

Philip the Arab

Millennial emperor — Celebrated Rome's traditional millennium in 248.

249–251 · Crisis of the Third Century

Decius

Frontier emperor — Died fighting the Goths at Abrittus.

270–275 · Crisis of the Third Century

Aurelian

Restorer — Reunified the empire after years of fragmentation.

284–305 · Tetrarchy

Diocletian

Reformer — Created the Tetrarchy and transformed imperial government.

306–337 · Constantinian

Constantine I

Constantine the Great — Defeated his rivals, favored Christianity and founded Constantinople.

361–363 · Constantinian

Julian

Last pagan emperor — Attempted to restore traditional Roman religious practices.

379–395 · Theodosian

Theodosius I

Last sole emperor — Last ruler to govern both halves before permanent separation of imperial courts.

393–423 · Theodosian

Honorius

Western emperor — Western emperor during the sack of Rome in 410.

425–455 · Theodosian

Valentinian III

Late western emperor — Ruled amid increasing fragmentation and military politics.

467–472 · Late Western Empire

Anthemius

Late western emperor — One of the final emperors of the western court.

475–476 · Late Western Empire

Romulus Augustulus

Traditional last western emperor — Deposed by Odoacer in 476, the conventional date for the end of the West.
